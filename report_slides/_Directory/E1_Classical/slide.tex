Establishing that is the first result rather than a preliminary. Cubic ($Q_3$)
discretisation, four uniform refinement cycles, outer iteration to a relative residual
tolerance of $10^{-10}$:

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\raggedright
\textbf{The discretisation converges at the expected rates.} Halving the mesh size divides
the $H^1$ error by $7.27$, $7.79$, $7.94$ and the $L^2$ error by $14.5$, $15.4$, $15.8$
--- observed orders $2.86\to2.99$ and $3.86\to3.98$, the optimal $\mathcal{O}(h^p)$ and
$\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1})$ for cubic elements.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\raggedright
\textbf{The multigrid preconditioner is doing its job.} The iteration count barely moves
--- $12$, $13$, $14$ --- while the mesh is refined three times and the system grows
sixty-fourfold, from $144$ to $9216$ unknowns. This is mesh-independence.
(\alert{Cycle~0 has a single level}, so its coarse solve is exact and CG terminates in one
step; it is not evidence either way.)
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{1mm}
\begin{block}{The constraint this puts on any claim}
A learned smoother cannot distinguish itself by fixing a failure of scalability that does
not occur. What it can be asked to do is reduce the \emph{constant}: fewer iterations to
the same tolerance, or the same count at lower cost per iteration. The reverse also holds,
and it is part of the result --- a learned smoother that shows no gain here has not been
shown useless, because the baseline it failed to beat was not weak.
\end{block}
