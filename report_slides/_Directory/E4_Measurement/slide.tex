\begin{block}{Answer: yes --- and it is the best single-step method in the table}
\raggedright
With a branch wide enough to represent the complement correction,
$\muF=0.1456$, $\muC=1.0928$, $\muC/\muF=7.50$, which places it first of the six methods on
complement reduction:
DeepONet $0.146<$ SymGS $0.189<$ GS $0.366<$ damped Jacobi $0.425<$ Jacobi $0.700$. It
removes $85\%$ of the complement's $A$-energy in one step --- better than symmetric
Gauss--Seidel --- and \emph{no sample is amplified} ($\mathbb{P}(\rF>1)=0$, worst case
$\max\rF=0.320$).
\end{block}

\bre
SSOR and symmetric Gauss--Seidel coincide because $\omega=1$ \emph{is} symmetric GS for
this sweep; the table shows them agreeing to every printed digit, which is a check on the
implementation rather than a coincidence. The network and damped Jacobi both have a free
parameter selected on the \emph{same} validation metric and scored on test, so the
comparison is symmetric. The ridge and rank-$k$ rows are \emph{diagnostics, not
competitors}: without them there is nothing to say whether $\muF=0.15$ is good, bad, or
exactly what the architecture permits.
