\frametitle{Motivation: PDEs on Curved Surfaces}
\small

\begin{columns}[T]

%=====================================================================
% left: what the problem is, and the two demands it makes
%=====================================================================
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textbf{The problem}

Surfaces and complex geometries are the rule, not the exception: biological
membranes, vascular networks, thin shells, material interfaces.

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Two demands, and they compete}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \alert{Geometry}. The discretisation must represent the surface
        faithfully; a geometry that is only approximately right poisons every
        step that follows it.
  \item \alert{Scale}. The resulting systems are large and sparse. A solver
        whose cost grows faster than the number of unknowns is unusable,
        however accurate the discretisation.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}

%=====================================================================
% right: the classical answer
%=====================================================================
\begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
\textbf{The classical answer}

\begin{block}{Two ingredients}
\alert{Finite elements} supply the geometry; \alert{multigrid} supplies the
solver.
\end{block}

Multigrid exploits the \alert{separation of scales} in the error:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a \emph{smoothing} iteration removes the oscillatory part;
  \item a \emph{coarse-grid correction} removes the smooth part that the
        smoother cannot reach.
\end{itemize}

Applied recursively over a mesh hierarchy this reaches \alert{optimal
complexity}: the work to reduce the residual by a fixed factor is proportional
to the number of unknowns.
\end{column}

\end{columns}

\begin{block}{Where it becomes difficult}
The efficiency depends on the \emph{design} of the components --- smoother,
restriction and prolongation, coarse-grid strategy. With a \alert{variable
coefficient}, on \alert{adaptively refined meshes}, or where the geometry is
known only through a \alert{point cloud}, keeping convergence uniform is hard,
and the performance of a classical cycle degrades substantially.
\end{block}
